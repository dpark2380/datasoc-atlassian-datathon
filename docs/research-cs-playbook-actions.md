# Customer success playbook research: actions for the four risk categories

Research question: what do real B2B SaaS customer success teams and health-scoring
platforms actually do for onboarding-risk, high-value-disengaged, and
lower-stakes-declining accounts, beyond generic email/call outreach. Sources below
are vendor documentation and named practitioner content from Gainsight, ChurnZero,
Totango, HubSpot, and Atlassian's own community and blog content.

## 1. Onboarding-risk accounts (new customers, low adoption)

Gainsight's own onboarding process is milestone-based and multi-channel, not a
single check-in call. It runs a kickoff call where a project manager or CSM sets
the plan and assigns pre-work, then tracks the account against standard
milestones for its segment, using a report to flag accounts falling behind
schedule (Gainsight, "Customer Onboarding Template," gainsight.com/blog/customer-onboarding-template).
The same process includes a structured adoption workshop where the customer
implements roughly 60% of the product's functionality in a guided session, not
left to self-discovery (Gainsight, "Best-in-Class Onboarding," gainsight.com/customer-success-best-practices/best-in-class-onboarding-make-it-happen-using-gainsight).

Concrete actions, cited:

- Milestone-based onboarding tracking with a named owner. Assign each new
  account a project owner (CSM or onboarding specialist) who runs a kickoff call,
  assigns customer-side pre-work, and tracks completion against segment-specific
  milestones (e.g. first workflow created, first integration connected, first
  team invited) rather than a single "did we speak to them" checkbox. Source:
  Gainsight customer onboarding template and process description
  (gainsight.com/blog/customer-onboarding-template).
- Structured adoption workshop, not a generic training call. A scheduled
  session with a specific completion target (e.g. "implement the core workflows
  this account needs") rather than an open-ended demo. Source: Gainsight,
  "Best-in-Class Onboarding" (gainsight.com/customer-success-best-practices/best-in-class-onboarding-make-it-happen-using-gainsight).
- In-app guided walkthroughs plus a 30-60-90 day feedback survey. Gainsight
  combines in-app messaging and product walkthroughs during onboarding with a
  structured 30/60/90-day survey to catch stalled accounts on a fixed schedule
  rather than waiting for a support ticket or a renewal date. Source: Gainsight,
  "Customer Onboarding Template" (gainsight.com/blog/customer-onboarding-template).
- Automated low-adoption alerts routed to a CSM before renewal, not after.
  HubSpot's customer success workspace flags low product adoption on accounts
  approaching renewal and triggers both a customer-facing nudge and an internal
  CSM alert, so the intervention happens while there's still time to fix it.
  Source: HubSpot, "Customer success renewals: 8 top strategies that prevent
  churn" (blog.hubspot.com/service/customer-success-renewals).

## 2. High-value, high-embeddedness accounts showing disengagement

The consistent finding across CS platforms is that the top account tier gets a
named human escalation path, not just a heavier version of the same email.

- Executive Business Review (EBR/QBR), reserved for top-tier accounts.
  Practitioner consensus (Gainsight, ChurnZero, Vitally, and the Customer
  Success Collective all publish near-identical guidance) is that an EBR is a
  quarterly, exec-to-exec meeting that reviews ROI delivered, surfaces risk
  early, and discusses renewal or expansion, and is explicitly reserved for the
  highest-value segment of the customer base rather than run for everyone.
  Sources: Gainsight, "Quarterly Business Review (QBR)"
  (gainsight.com/blog/quarterly-business-review-qbr); ChurnZero, "Quarterly
  business reviews: a blueprint for success" (churnzero.com/blog/quarterly-business-reviews).
- Leadership presence and technical resource pairing for tech-heavy
  accounts. ChurnZero's QBR guidance recommends including a leadership-team
  attendee for top accounts "as a sign of commitment and respect," and pairing
  a technical resource from the vendor side with one from the customer side
  when the review will get into implementation detail. Source: ChurnZero,
  "Quarterly business reviews: a blueprint for success"
  (churnzero.com/blog/quarterly-business-reviews).
- Human escalation triggered by specific deterioration signals, not a
  generic "at risk" flag. ChurnZero names concrete escalation triggers for
  high-value accounts: an executive sponsor departing, a health-score drop
  within 60 days of renewal, negative sentiment recurring across multiple
  touchpoints, or a support escalation signalling relationship risk. These
  route to a human, not an automated play. Source: ChurnZero, "What is a
  Customer Health Score in SaaS" (churnzero.com/churnopedia/health-score) and
  ChurnZero renewal-risk material (churnzero.com/customer-success-software/forecast-renewals-predict-churn).
- Joint CSM-plus-account-owner engagement on the highest-value accounts.
  Gainsight's own high-touch strategic account practice runs an initial
  executive alignment meeting during onboarding and recurring Executive
  Check-In and Executive Business Review triggers through the adoption phase,
  run jointly by the CSM and account leadership rather than the CSM alone.
  Source: Gainsight, "How We Drive Success with High-Touch Customers Using
  Gainsight" (gainsight.com/blog/how-we-drive-success-with-high-touch-customers-using-gainsight).

## 3. Lower-stakes declining accounts (heavy touch not cost-justified)

ChurnZero publishes a dedicated framework for exactly this tier, explicitly
built around automated plays rather than CSM time, and it is more specific than
"send an email."

- Usage-triggered play sequence, not a single email. ChurnZero's low- and
  mid-touch feature adoption play is a multi-step sequence: an automated
  trigger fires when usage of a specific feature drops or stalls, an email
  goes out naming that the account was reviewed and identifying the specific
  underused feature and its benefit, an in-app message reinforces the same
  message one to two days later, and a follow-up email recaps any call and the
  advantages of adopting the feature. Source: ChurnZero, "A Quick-Start Guide
  to Low- and Mid-Touch Feature Adoption"
  (churnzero.com/blog/quick-start-guide-to-low-and-mid-touch-feature-adoption).
- Positive-reinforcement triggers, not only recovery triggers. The same
  ChurnZero play also fires a congratulatory in-app message when a customer
  crosses a positive usage threshold, paired with related self-serve
  educational content (tips, use cases) to consolidate the habit rather than
  waiting until the account declines again. Source: ChurnZero, "A Quick-Start
  Guide to Low- and Mid-Touch Feature Adoption"
  (churnzero.com/blog/quick-start-guide-to-low-and-mid-touch-feature-adoption).
- Complimentary training tied to the specific unused feature. ChurnZero
  documents automatically offering a short complimentary training session
  scoped to the specific feature the account has stopped using, rather than a
  generic "check-in call." Source: ChurnZero, "How to scale customer success
  with tiered, AI engagement" (churnzero.com/blog/how-to-scale-customer-success-with-ai).
- Tech-touch segment handling frees CSM time for the tier that needs it.
  HubSpot's tech-touch (digital-led) model routes this segment to automated
  in-app tips, triggered email sequences, and self-service articles by
  default, keeping human CSM time for the high-value tier. Source: HubSpot,
  "Customer success models: how to build one that lasts"
  (blog.hubspot.com/service/customer-success-model).

## 4. Atlassian-specific adoption and disengagement practice

Atlassian's own community and product content describes a champions-led model
distinct from a CSM-led enterprise model, which matters because the team's
model spans Jira, Confluence, Trello, Bitbucket, and Loom.

- Jira Adoption Guide, published by Atlassian. Atlassian publishes a
  structured adoption guide aimed at internal product champions (not just
  CSMs), covering kickstart team training via onboarding sessions or
  lunch-and-learns, workflow and automation optimization guidance, and
  real-world success stories from other Jira champions used as social proof
  inside the guide itself. Source: Atlassian Community, "Empower your team:
  Introducing the Jira Adoption Guide"
  (community.atlassian.com/forums/Jira-articles/Empower-your-team-Introducing-the-Jira-Adoption-Guide/ba-p/3142266).
- A named champions program. Atlassian's own blog describes deliberately
  identifying and supporting internal product champions at customer
  organizations: involving them early in pilots, running dedicated enablement
  sessions for them, giving them a private channel, and recognizing their
  contribution publicly, treating grassroots internal champions as the
  adoption mechanism rather than the vendor CSM alone. Source: Atlassian
  Community forums, "Customer Success" group
  (community.atlassian.com/forums/Customer-Success/gh-p/CustomerSuccess), and
  ServiceRocket's Atlassian-focused adoption content, an Atlassian Solution
  Partner publishing under Atlassian's own community channels
  (blog.servicerocket.com/adoption/topic/customer-success).
- Account prioritization via Jira Product Discovery. Atlassian's own
  Customer Success team uses Jira Product Discovery internally to prioritize
  which accounts get attention, i.e. Atlassian runs its own CS org on its own
  product for account triage. This is a credible, checkable precedent for
  "Atlassian CS already does structured account prioritization with its own
  tools," directly relevant to a pitch aimed at Atlassian. Source: Atlassian
  Community, "How Atlassian's Customer Success team uses Jira Product
  Discovery for account prioritization"
  (community.atlassian.com/forums/Jira-Product-Discovery-articles/How-Atlassian-s-Customer-Success-team-uses-Jira-Product/ba-p/3033606).

This gives the deck a credible hook: Atlassian already runs a champions-led
adoption model and already uses its own product (Jira Product Discovery) for
CS account triage. The recommendation below builds on both rather than
proposing something foreign to how Atlassian's CS org already works.

## 5. What health-scoring platforms actually combine to decide "at-risk"

This checks the team's model (usage decline vs. tier peers, tenure,
embeddedness) against what Gainsight and ChurnZero say their health scores
are built from.

- Gainsight builds its health score from a mix of NPS, support ticket
  volume, onboarding/product experience surveys, sentiment, and the
  customer's own business health, and separately warns that product usage
  must be measured on both depth and breadth of feature adoption, not
  login count or time-in-app alone, since heavy but narrow usage can mean the
  product is confusing rather than sticky. Source: Gainsight, "Customer
  Health Score Explained: Metrics, Models & Tools"
  (gainsight.com/blog/customer-health-scores). Gainsight's Scorecards module
  also weights CSM sentiment as a manual override signal alongside usage and
  survey data (gainsight.com/blog/building-the-foundation-for-customer-visibility-with-usage-data-and-health-scoring).
- ChurnZero splits health-score inputs into quantitative factors (login
  history, feature engagement, support ticket volume and severity, NPS/CSAT/CES)
  and qualitative factors (relationship quality, perceived ROI, competitor
  risk, feature requests). Source: ChurnZero, "What is a Customer Health Score
  in SaaS" (churnzero.com/churnopedia/health-score).

Gap check against the team's model: the team's model (usage decline relative
to plan-tier peers, tenure, embeddedness/collaborator-integration count) covers
usage and account structure well, but neither Gainsight nor ChurnZero treats
usage and tenure alone as sufficient. Both platforms rely additionally on
support ticket sentiment/volume and survey-based sentiment (NPS/CSAT)
as independent signals, on the reasoning that an account can look
usage-healthy while the relationship is deteriorating (a departing champion,
an unresolved support escalation) with no usage-metric footprint yet. Given
the earlier repo history shows the team explicitly dropped fabricated
ticket/sentiment logic once it couldn't be grounded in real data, the honest
framing for the deck is: this is a known gap versus industry practice, not an
oversight, and the model should say so rather than paper over it with an
invented sentiment score.

## 6. Proposed upgraded actions for the team's four categories

1. Monitor Only. No action. Unchanged.

2. New & Struggling gets a milestone-tracked onboarding review, not a generic
   check-in. Assign a named CSM owner who runs a structured adoption session
   scoped to the specific Jira/Confluence/Trello/Bitbucket/Loom workflows this
   account has set up but not adopted, tracked against a 30/60/90-day
   milestone checklist rather than a single call. This mirrors Gainsight's
   documented onboarding milestone process (gainsight.com/blog/customer-onboarding-template)
   and can reuse Atlassian's own published Jira Adoption Guide content as the
   session material (community.atlassian.com/forums/Jira-articles/Empower-your-team-Introducing-the-Jira-Adoption-Guide/ba-p/3142266)
   instead of building new collateral from scratch.

3. High-Value Disengaged gets an Executive Business Review with joint CSM
   and account leadership escalation. Trigger a quarterly, exec-to-exec review
   scoped to this account's actual ROI and adoption data (not a template
   deck), jointly owned by the CSM and account leadership, following the
   structure ChurnZero and Gainsight document for top-tier accounts
   (churnzero.com/blog/quarterly-business-reviews, gainsight.com/blog/how-we-drive-success-with-high-touch-customers-using-gainsight).
   Given Atlassian's own CS team already uses Jira Product Discovery to
   prioritize accounts internally (community.atlassian.com/forums/Jira-Product-Discovery-articles/How-Atlassian-s-Customer-Success-team-uses-Jira-Product/ba-p/3033606),
   this escalation can be logged and tracked the same way, making it a
   process extension rather than a new tool.

4. Established & Low Engagement gets a usage-triggered feature-specific play, not
   a generic email. Replace the scheduled check-in with an automated play
   scoped to the specific feature or product (of the five in the suite) whose
   usage has dropped: an email naming that specific feature and its value,
   an in-app nudge one to two days later, and a complimentary short training
   session on that feature if usage doesn't recover. This directly follows
   ChurnZero's documented low/mid-touch play structure
   (churnzero.com/blog/quick-start-guide-to-low-and-mid-touch-feature-adoption),
   costs no CSM time up front, and only escalates to a human if the automated
   play doesn't move usage.
